<Callout variant="tip" title="Key Takeaways">
- **Form and vet your team before the event**: the 2am dropout sinks teams
- **Pre-stage tooling and scaffold**: environment failures waste hours
- **Define the MVP in one sentence**: if you can't, your scope is too broad
- **Budget time for the presentation**: a great demo beats a great build
- **Sleep 3-4 hours**: the no-sleep culture destroys performance
- **Plan for demo failure**: have a screen recording as insurance
- **Move and eat real food**: a hackathon is an extreme sedentary event; survive it physically
</Callout>

Most hackathon teams don't lose because their idea was bad. They lose because they ran out of time, fought about scope, had a teammate disappear at 3am, or delivered a broken demo. The difference between a winning team and an also-ran is rarely talent — it's preparation and execution. This guide covers everything you control before and during a hackathon that determines whether you finish with a working demo or a half-built GitHub repo.

## Before the Hackathon

### Team Formation Strategies

Your team is 70% of your outcome. Form it deliberately, not at kickoff.

**Ideal team composition (4 people):**
- **1 frontend/generalist**: builds the demo-able UI
- **1 backend/integration**: wires services together
- **1 ML/specialist**: the hard technical part
- **1 presenter/polisher**: owns the demo and slide deck

If you're a team of 2-3, combine roles — but someone must own the demo. A team where everyone codes and no one prepares the presentation loses to a team with a weaker build and a great demo.

**Vet commitment before registering:**
- Confirm everyone can attend the full duration
- Agree on [sleep strategy](/articles/sleep-optimization-shift-workers) in advance (see Health section)
- Set a "no silent quitting" rule — if someone needs to drop, they say so at hour 6, not hour 18

**Where to find teammates:**
- Your existing network (best — you know their reliability)
- The hackathon's Discord/Slack (most events have one pre-event)
- Local meetups (see our [meetup guide](/blog/local-tech-meetups-finding-guide))

### Idea Brainstorming

Don't wait until kickoff to brainstorm. Have 3-5 candidate ideas ready, then pick at the event based on the theme and [judging criteria](/articles/hackathon-judging-criteria-guide).

**Good hackathon ideas have:**
- A clear demo path (you can show the result in 4 minutes)
- A "wow moment" (one feature that makes judges react)
- Feasibility in the time available (if it needs 3 weeks, it's not a hackathon idea)
- A fallback (a simpler version you can ship if the ambitious version stalls)

**Bad hackathon ideas:**
- Anything requiring "we just need to train a model first" (training takes too long)
- Anything where the value only appears at scale (judges can't see scale in a demo)
- Anything you've already built (judges and rules usually exclude prior work)

### Tooling Prep

Pre-stage everything before the event:
- **Repo scaffolded**: monorepo or polyrepo, CI/CD skipped (no time), basic structure ready
- **Accounts and API keys**: OpenAI/Anthropic, cloud provider, any sponsor APIs — create keys now, not at 2am when you hit rate limits
- **Local dev environments**: everyone confirms their setup works before arrival
- **Demo infrastructure**: a deployment target (Vercel, Railway, a cloud VM) ready to go
- **Template slide deck**: title, problem, solution, demo, tech stack — fill in as you build

<Callout type="info">
**The 2am failure:** The most common hackathon disaster is a teammate whose environment doesn't work and spends 4 hours debugging setup instead of building. Verify every teammate can run the scaffold before the event. No exceptions.
</Callout>

## Idea Selection and Scoping

### MVP Definition

Define your MVP in one sentence before you write any code: "By judging time, we will have [specific working feature] that demonstrates [specific value]."

If you can't state it in one sentence, your scope is too broad. Cut until you can.

### Judge Criteria Analysis

Read the judging rubric before finalizing your idea. [Hackathons](/articles/tech-hackathons-2026-calendar) weight differently:
- **Technical difficulty**: favor ambitious architecture, hard algorithms
- **Impact/usefulness**: favor real-world problem, clear user
- **Polish/design**: favor a beautiful, working demo
- **Sponsor prizes**: favor use of a specific sponsor's API/tool

Align your idea to the rubric. A technically brilliant project loses a "polish"-weighted hackathon to a simpler, prettier one. Know the game you're playing.

### Scope Cutting

The most important skill in a hackathon: recognizing when to cut. Rules:
- **Cut at hour 8, not hour 18** — early cuts preserve the core; late cuts leave broken edges
- **Cut features, not the core demo path** — the "wow moment" stays; peripheral features go
- **Have a "v1" defined explicitly** — the minimum that demos well; everything else is "v2 if time"
- **When in doubt, cut** — unfinished features hurt more than missing features

## During the Event

### Time Allocation

A rough budget for a 24-hour hackathon:
- **Hour 0-2**: finalize idea, divide work, scaffold (already done if you prepped)
- **Hour 2-14**: build the core — the demo path end to end
- **Hour 14-18**: polish, fix the demo-breaking bugs, deploy
- **Hour 18-22**: build the presentation, rehearse the demo
- **Hour 22-24**: sleep (yes, really — see Health), final rehearsal
- **Hour 24**: present

The teams that fail allocate all 24 hours to building and zero to presenting. A working project with a bad demo loses to a half-working project with a great demo.

### Division of Labor

Parallelize from hour 1:
- **Backend and frontend work simultaneously** against an agreed API contract
- **The presenter starts the slide deck at hour 12** — not hour 22
- **One person owns deployment** — getting it live early exposes integration bugs
- **The ML/specialist works the hardest part first** — if it's going to fail, know by hour 8

### Checkpoint Reviews

Stop the team every 4 hours for a 10-minute sync:
- **What's working?**
- **What's blocked?**
- **Are we on the demo path or distracted?**
- **Do we need to cut?**

These feel like interruptions but save hours of misaligned work. A team that syncs every 4 hours out-executes a team that goes heads-down for 24 hours.

## Presentation and Demo

### Demo Script

Write the demo as a script before rehearsing:
1. **"Here's the problem"** (30 sec) — set up the pain
2. **"Here's our solution"** (30 sec) — one sentence
3. **"Let me show you"** (2-3 min) — the live demo, narrated
4. **"Here's what's under the hood"** (30-60 sec) — the technical depth
5. **"What's next"** (15 sec) — future vision, shows it's real

Total: 4-5 minutes. Practice it until you can do it without notes.

### Slide Design

- **Max 6 slides** — title, problem, solution, demo (you'll switch to the live app), architecture, team
- **One idea per slide**
- **Architecture diagram** showing the tech stack — judges care about this
- **No code on slides** unless it's a single, highlighted line

### Handling Live Demo Failures

Live demos fail. Plan for it:
- **Have a screen recording of the demo working** — if live fails, play the recording and narrate
- **Know your fallback** — "if the live demo breaks, I'll show you the recording"
- **Don't panic-aologize** — calmly switch to the recording; judges have seen worse
- **Rehearse the failure** — actually practice switching to the recording so it's smooth

The recording isn't cheating — it's insurance. Most winning teams have one.

## Health and Survival

### Sleep Strategy

The "no sleep" hackathon culture is macho nonsense that destroys performance. The evidence is clear: after 17 hours awake, cognitive performance equals legal alcohol impairment. A team that sleeps 4 hours beats a team that sleeps 0.

**Recommended strategy for 24h events:**
- **One 3-4 hour sleep block** between 2am-6am for the whole team
- **The presenter sleeps the most** — they're on stage; they need to be sharp
- **Set alarms** — oversleeping is as bad as not sleeping

### Movement Breaks

A hackathon is an extreme version of the sedentary, high-focus work ActiveNerds covers all year. Apply the same principles, intensified:

<Callout type="tip">
**Hackathon movement protocol:**
1. Stand up every 90 minutes — even 60 seconds helps
2. Walk during any team discussion over 5 minutes
3. 20-20-20 rule every hour (look 20 feet away for 20 seconds)
4. Hydrate aggressively — caffeine dehydrates and you'll forget water
5. Do desk stretches at your workstation between commits
</Callout>

See our [micro-exercise routines](/blog/micro-exercise-routines-developers) and [movement snack checklist](/blog/movement-snack-checklist-desk-workers) for the full routines.

### Nutrition Tips

- **Eat real food, not just energy drinks and pizza** — blood sugar crashes kill focus
- **Protein every 4-6 hours** — sustained energy, not spikes
- **Limit caffeine after midnight** — it'll wreck the sleep block you need
- **Have snacks at the workstation** — you won't leave to eat unless food is present

### Post-Event Recovery

- **Sleep 8+ hours the next night** — you're in sleep debt; repay it
- **Take a real day off** — don't jump back into work the next morning
- **Hydrate and move** — a long walk the day after clears the stiffness
- **Reflect**: what worked, what didn't, what to do differently next time
