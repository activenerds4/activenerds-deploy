Hackathons are where skills compound. You ship something in 24-48 hours that would take a month at work, meet people you'd never encounter in your Slack, and occasionally walk away with a prize or a job offer. But the good ones fill up fast and deadlines sneak up. This is a curated calendar of the best tech hackathons in 2026, focused on AI/ML, Kubernetes, and DevSecOps — the fields ActiveNerds readers actually work in.

## How to Use This Calendar

### Filtering by Region

Events are grouped by region (North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific, Virtual). [Virtual events](/articles/virtual-tech-events-online-meetups) are open globally and often the best entry point if you can't travel. Most in-person events offer travel stipends for accepted teams — check the FAQ before dismissing one as too far.

### Registration Deadlines

Registration deadlines typically close 2-4 weeks before the event. Some popular events (notably university hackathons) close even earlier. The dates below are event dates — register early. I've noted registration deadlines where they're published.

### Skill Level Guide

- **Beginner-friendly**: mentorship-heavy, no experience required, good for first-timers
- **Intermediate**: assumes you can build a working prototype end-to-end
- **Advanced**: competitive, often with qualifying rounds or team requirements

## Hackathon Prep Tips

### Team Formation

- **Form before the event**: scrambling for a team at kickoff wastes hours
- **Mix skills**: one frontend, one backend, one ML/data, one presenter is a strong 4-person team
- **Vet commitment**: confirm everyone can stay the full duration — a teammate who leaves at hour 12 sinks the team

### Idea Validation

- **Scope to the demo**: judges see a 4-minute demo, not your codebase. Build what demos well.
- **Check the judging criteria**: some hackathons weight technical difficulty, others weight impact or polish. Align your idea to the rubric.
- **Have a fallback**: your ambitious idea will hit a wall at 2am. Have a simpler version ready to fall back to.

### Staying Active During 24h Events

This is where ActiveNerds readers should pay attention. A 24-hour hackathon is an extreme version of the sedentary, high-focus work life we cover all year. Survival tactics:

<Callout type="tip">
**Movement protocol for 24h hackathons:**
1. Stand up every 90 minutes — even for 60 seconds
2. Walk during any team discussion longer than 5 minutes
3. Do the 20-20-20 rule every hour (look 20 feet away for 20 seconds)
4. Hydrate aggressively — caffeine dehydrates and you'll forget to drink water
5. Take one 20-minute nap between 2am-5am if you're flagging; it's more effective than pushing through
</Callout>
