On-call is a marathon dressed up as a sprint. Each page feels urgent, but a rotation is days or weeks of sustained vigilance — and the engineers who burn out are the ones who treat every shift like an emergency. Sustainable pacing isn't about working less during on-call; it's about managing your energy so you're still functional on day 5, not just day 1. This guide covers the evidence-based protocols I've used and taught for sustaining performance through on-call rotations without burning out.

## The On-Call Energy Problem

### Alert Fatigue

Every alert demands attention, but not every alert deserves the same response. Alert fatigue — the desensitization that comes from too many low-value pages — is both a symptom and a cause of unsustainable on-call. When everything is urgent, nothing is, and you spend the rotation in a state of chronic low-grade stress.

The systemic fix is alert tuning (which is its own engineering project), but the personal fix is triage discipline: not every page needs an immediate, full-adrenaline response.

### Sleep Disruption Impact

A single night page can cost you 2-3 hours of sleep — the page itself, the resolution, and the post-alert adrenaline that prevents re-sleep. Over a rotation, this accumulates into the cognitive equivalent of legal alcohol impairment after a few days. Sleep debt is the single biggest driver of [on-call burnout](/articles/burnout-prevention-on-call).

### Cumulative Stress

On-call stress compounds. Day 1 is manageable; day 5 with sleep debt and accumulated alerts is a different animal. The engineers who sustain on-call are the ones who plan for the cumulative load, not just the individual pages.

<Callout type="warning">
If you're on a rotation longer than a week and getting night pages, you're in a system that's actively harming you. No individual pacing strategy fully compensates for a broken rotation. See our [burnout prevention guide](/blog/burnout-prevention-on-call) for the systemic fixes to push for.
</Callout>

## Energy Management Framework

### Ultradian Mapping to Shifts

Your brain works in 90-minute cycles (ultradian rhythms). Mapping your on-call day to these cycles preserves energy:

- **90-minute focus blocks** for actual work (when not paged)
- **10-20 minute breaks** between blocks — movement, not screens
- **No more than 3-4 deep focus blocks per day** on-call — you're already carrying vigilance load

The mistake is trying to do a full day's deep work while on-call. On-call is already a [cognitive load](/articles/focus-management-long-training-runs); budget your work capacity accordingly.

### Strategic Caffeine

Caffeine is the on-call engineer's tool and trap. Used strategically:
- **Delay first caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking** — this lets natural cortisol peak, and avoids afternoon crash
- **Stop caffeine 10-12 hours before bedtime** — caffeine's half-life is 5-6 hours; a 2pm coffee is still half-active at midnight
- **Use caffeine before a known hard window** (e.g., a planned deploy), not reactively after every page
- **Don't caffeine a night page** — it'll extend the sleep disruption; better to be tired than wired

### Recovery Windows

Build recovery into the rotation, not just after it:
- **A 20-minute nap after a night page** — restores alertness better than caffeine
- **A real [lunch break](/articles/lunch-break-workouts-developers), away from the screen** — don't eat at your desk while monitoring
- **A walk between focus blocks** — movement metabolizes stress hormones
- **A hard stop on work at a set time** (when not paged) — on-call ≠ always working

## Sleep Protection

### Pre-Sleep Protocols

Protecting sleep before it's disrupted is easier than recovering it:
- **A 30-60 minute wind-down** before bed — no screens, no work
- **Cool, dark room** — temperature matters more than you think
- **No caffeine after the early afternoon** — see above
- **Phone on Do Not Disturb**, with only the paging system as an exception
- **A "ready to respond" ritual** — laptop charged, paging app tested, so a page doesn't require setup

### Post-Page Re-Entry

When you're paged at night, the goal is to respond well and get back to sleep:
- **Minimum light** — a single dim light, not overheads; light suppresses melatonin
- **Don't check the time** — "how much sleep am I losing?" creates anxiety that prevents re-sleep
- **Resolve or hand off, don't rabbit-hole** — if it's complex, stabilize and hand off or page secondary
- **No screens after resolution** — don't check Slack "just in case"; go back to bed
- **A brief wind-down** — 5-10 minutes of non-screen time before trying to sleep

### Sleep Banking Before Rotation

You can't fully stock up on sleep, but you can reduce the deficit:
- **The week before a rotation**: prioritize 8+ hours nightly
- **Pay down existing sleep debt** — don't enter on-call already depleted
- **Adjust your schedule** to the rotation's hours if possible (e.g., earlier bedtime if you'll get early pages)

<Callout type="tip">
**The 20-minute nap:** If paged at night, a nap the next day restores more than extra caffeine. Set an alarm for 20 minutes (not longer — longer hits deep sleep and you'll wake groggy). It's the single most effective on-call recovery tool.
</Callout>

## Movement and Nutrition

### Post-Alert Movement

After a stressful alert, your body is full of cortisol and adrenaline. Movement metabolizes these stress hormones — sitting with them leaves you wired and depleted:
- **A 10-minute walk after a significant incident** — physically separate from the screen
- **Standing desk up for 30 minutes after a page** — changes posture, breaks the stress posture
- **Desk stretches if you can't leave** — see our [desk stretch guide](/blog/desk-stretches-coding-sessions)

The instinct after a page is to stay glued to the screen "in case it recurs." Counter it — the movement is what lets you handle the next page well.

### On-Call Nutrition Prep

On-call nutrition fails because you're too busy/stressed to eat well, so you grab junk:
- **Prep food before the rotation** — meals ready to heat, not decisions to make
- **Protein every 4-6 hours** — stabilizes energy and mood
- **Limit sugar and simple carbs** — the crash worsens stress
- **Keep water at your desk** — dehydration impairs concentration within 2 hours
- **Have a "page food" stash** — nuts, jerky, fruit; things you can eat one-handed while triaging

### Hydration Tracking

Dehydration mimics stress symptoms — fatigue, irritability, poor concentration — and compounds the real stress of on-call:
- **A water bottle you can see** — if it's visible, you'll drink it
- **Drink after every page** — a [habit trigger](/articles/desk-stretch-timer-app-comparison) that ensures hydration
- **Electrolytes if you're sweating through incidents** — stress sweat depletes them

## Post-Rotation Recovery

### Decompression Rituals

The end of a rotation isn't the end of the stress — your nervous system is still primed. Decompress deliberately:
- **A real day off after the rotation** — not a "low-intensity" work day, an actual day off
- **A longer walk or workout** — metabolize the accumulated stress hormones
- **Sleep recovery** — 8+ hours for several nights; you're paying down debt
- **A transition ritual** — something that signals "off-call" (a meal, a walk, a workout)

### Handoff Quality

A good handoff protects both you and the next on-call:
- **A written handoff doc** — open incidents, ongoing issues, what to watch
- **A live handoff call** — 15 minutes to walk through the state
- **No dumping unresolved stress** — hand off the work, not the anxiety

### Long-Term Sustainability Check

After each rotation, a brief self-assessment:
- **Did I sleep adequately?** (If no, the rotation design is the problem)
- **Did I have any genuine [recovery time](/articles/managing-context-switching-engineering)?**
- **How many pages were low-value?** (Push to tune these)
- **Would I be sustainable for 3 more months of this?** (If no, something needs to change)

If the answer to the last question is no, that's not a personal failing — it's a signal to push for systemic change. See our [burnout prevention guide](/blog/burnout-prevention-on-call) for how to frame that conversation with your team.

## Summary & Next Steps

Sustainable on-call is partly personal discipline and mostly system design. The personal protocols in this guide help you survive a reasonable rotation; they can't make an unreasonable one sustainable. Use them, and use your post-rotation assessment to push for the systemic changes — shorter rotations, follow-the-sun, secondary on-call, alert tuning — that make on-call humane.
