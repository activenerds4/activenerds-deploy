Standing desks are everywhere in tech offices. But the hype has outpaced the evidence. Standing isn't the opposite of sitting — it's just a different kind of sedentary behavior if you do it wrong. This article gives you the honest benefits, the real drawbacks, and a transition protocol that won't leave you with sore knees and aching feet.

## The Evidence

### What Studies Actually Show

The research on standing desks is more nuanced than the marketing suggests:

- **Calorie burn:** Standing burns about 8-10 more calories per hour than sitting. That's roughly the equivalent of a carrot. Standing is not a weight-loss strategy.
- **Posture and back pain:** Some [studies show](/articles/blue-light-glasses-effectiveness) reduced back pain in [standing desk](/articles/standing-desk-transition-schedule) users, but only when they alternate between sitting and standing. Standing all day causes as much back pain as sitting all day.
- **Blood sugar:** Post-meal standing improves glucose response compared to post-meal sitting. This is the strongest evidence in favor of standing desks.
- **Productivity:** Studies show no significant difference in cognitive performance between sitting and standing. The productivity benefit is from movement, not posture.

<Callout type="info">
The honest summary: standing desks don't burn meaningful calories, don't make you smarter, and don't fix your back if you just stand all day. They do improve blood sugar regulation and they enable the thing that actually matters — alternating between positions.
</Callout>

### Calorie Burn Reality

| Activity | Calories/hour (70kg person) |
|----------|---------------------------|
| Sitting | 65 |
| Standing | 75 |
| Walking (slow) | 200 |
| [Walking desk](/articles/walking-pad-under-desk-review) (1 mph) | 150 |

The jump from sitting to standing is 10 calories/hour. The jump from standing to walking is 75-125 calories/hour. If your goal is metabolic health, the [standing desk](/articles/fitness-tracker-standing-desk-users) is a stepping stone to a walking desk, not the destination.

## Real Benefits

### Reduced Sitting Time

The primary benefit of a standing desk is that it breaks up long stretches of sitting. Even if you stand for only 2 hours of an 8-hour workday, you've reduced your sitting time by 25%. The health risk of sitting is about duration — 8 hours uninterrupted is worse than 8 hours broken up.

### Energy and Alertness

Many users report feeling more alert when standing, especially in the afternoon. This is likely because standing engages your core and leg muscles, increasing blood flow. It's not a caffeine replacement, but it helps with the post-lunch dip.

### Blood Sugar Regulation

The strongest evidence: standing for 30 minutes after a meal significantly reduces the post-meal blood sugar spike compared to sitting. For developers who eat lunch at their desks, this is the most compelling reason to have a standing desk.

## Common Mistakes

### Standing Too Long Too Fast

The #1 mistake is going from 8 hours of sitting to 8 hours of standing overnight. Your body isn't ready for it. Your feet, calves, and lower back need weeks to adapt.

<Warning>
If you stand for 4+ hours on your first day, you'll experience foot pain, calf tightness, and lower back ache. This is not "detoxing" — it's your body telling you to ease in. Most people who abandon standing desks do so in the first week because they went too hard.
</Warning>

### Poor Footwear

Standing in flat shoes on a hard floor is a recipe for pain. Your feet need support:
- Wear shoes with cushioning and arch support (not flat sandals or dress shoes with no sole)
- Or use an anti-fatigue mat (see below)
- Barefoot on hard floor is the worst option

### Static Standing vs Shifting

The healthiest way to stand is dynamically — shifting weight, rising onto toes, doing calf raises. Static standing (locked knees, motionless) causes blood pooling in your legs and is actually worse for circulation than sitting.

## Transition Protocol

### Week 1-2: Ramp-Up

Start with 30 minutes of standing per day, split into 2-3 sessions:

```
Day 1-3:  15 min morning, 15 min afternoon
Day 4-7:  30 min morning, 30 min afternoon
Day 8-10: 45 min morning, 45 min afternoon
Day 11-14: 1 hour morning, 1 hour afternoon
```

Stand after lunch — this is when the blood sugar benefit is greatest. Sit for deep work sessions when you need to focus intensely.

### Sit-Stand Ratio

The ideal ratio is not 50/50. Research suggests:

- **Best:** 2:1 sit-to-stand (e.g., 2 hours sitting, 1 hour standing, repeat)
- **Minimum:** 3:1 sit-to-stand
- **Avoid:** Anything more than 4:1 (you're not using the standing desk enough)

A practical pattern for developers:

```
9:00-11:00  Sit (deep work block)
11:00-11:30 Stand (email, code review, meetings)
11:30-12:30 Sit (deep work block)
12:30-13:00 Lunch
13:00-13:30 Stand (post-lunch — blood sugar benefit)
13:30-15:30 Sit (deep work block)
15:30-16:00 Stand (afternoon energy boost)
16:00-17:00 Sit (wrap up)
```

### Anti-Fatigue Mat Advice

An anti-fatigue mat is the single most important accessory for a standing desk. It reduces foot, knee, and lower back pain by providing cushioning and encouraging micro-movements:

- **Material:** Polyurethane foam (not cheap foam — it compresses and loses cushioning)
- **Thickness:** 3/4 inch minimum
- **Size:** Large enough to shift your stance comfortably
- **Budget:** $40-80 for a quality mat that lasts years

<Callout type="tip">
If you're on a budget, a yoga mat folded in half works as a temporary anti-fatigue mat. It's not as good, but it's better than standing on hard floor.
</Callout>

## Movement Integration

### Calf Raises While Standing

While standing at your desk, rise onto your toes and lower. 20 reps takes 30 seconds and pumps blood from your calves back to your heart. This is the single best "exercise snack" to do at a standing desk.

### Weight Shifting

Don't stand symmetrically. Shift your weight from one foot to the other every minute or two. This prevents the blood pooling that causes the "heavy legs" feeling.

### Mini Walks

The standing desk is a gateway to movement. Use your standing time as a trigger for a mini-walk — walk to get water, walk to a colleague's desk, walk around the office. Standing is better than sitting, but walking is better than standing.

## Summary

Standing desks are a tool, not a cure. The benefit comes from alternating between sitting and standing — not from standing all day. Transition slowly (2-week ramp-up), use an anti-fatigue mat, stand after meals for blood sugar benefits, and use standing time as a trigger for movement.
