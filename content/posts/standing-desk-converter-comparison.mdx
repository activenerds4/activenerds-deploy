A full standing desk costs $400-$1,500 and replaces your existing furniture. A [standing desk](/articles/monitor-arm-mount-standing-desk) converter costs $150-$400 and sits on top of what you already have. For renters, people with a desk they like, or anyone testing whether standing works for them, the converter is the pragmatic entry point. This guide compares the best options across stability, height range, and value.

## Why a Converter May Be Better

### Cost Advantage

The math is simple: a quality converter ($250-$350) is half to a third the cost of a quality electric standing desk ($600-$1,000). If you're unsure whether you'll stick with standing, the converter lets you test the hypothesis cheaply. If you love it, you can upgrade later. If you don't, you're out $250, not $1,000.

### No Furniture Replacement

Converters don't require ditching your existing desk. This matters for:
- **Renters** who can't justify buying furniture they'll move
- **People with a desk they like** (solid wood, specific size, sentimental)
- **Office workers** who can't swap company-issued furniture

### Apartment-Friendly

A converter adds height without adding footprint. In a small apartment where a second piece of furniture won't fit, the converter turns your existing desk into a sit-stand workstation overnight.

<Callout type="info">
**The trade-off:** converters raise only your monitor and keyboard — your desk surface stays at sitting height. You lose the full-surface standing workspace that a real standing desk provides. For most coders (monitor + keyboard + notebook), this is fine. For people who spread out papers or use a tablet while standing, it's limiting.
</Callout>

## Top Pick: Vari Converter

The Vari (formerly Varidesk) Converter is the most recognized name in the category and the most stable.

### Stability Rating

The Vari's spring-assisted lift mechanism and broad base make it the most wobble-free converter I've used. At full height (17.5 inches of lift), a 27-inch monitor barely trembles when you type. Cheaper converters flex visibly at full extension.

**Key features:**
- 17.5-inch height range
- Holds up to 35 lbs (monitor + laptop)
- Spring-assisted lift (two-handled)
- Built-in keyboard tray (12 x 5 inches)
- 11 height settings
- No assembly required

**Price:** $395

**Pros:**
- Rock-solid stability at full height
- Tool-free height adjustment
- Heavy-duty construction
- Works with dual monitors up to 27 inches

**Cons:**
- Heavy (47 lbs) — not something you'll move between desks
- Keyboard tray is compact (full keyboards with numpad overhang slightly)
- Premium price for a converter

### Height Range

The 17.5-inch range accommodates users from roughly 5'2" to 6'3" — broader than most converters. If you're outside that range, test before buying.

## Budget Pick: FlexiSpot

FlexiSpot offers the best value, with several models under $200.

### Price-to-Performance

The FlexiSpot M1B (and similar models) delivers 80% of the Vari's stability at half the price. The main compromise is a slightly narrower height range and more wobble at full extension.

**Key features:**
- 15-inch height range
- Holds up to 33 lbs
- Gas spring mechanism
- Built-in keyboard tray
- 12 height settings
- Tool-free assembly

**Price:** $170

**Pros:**
- Excellent value
- Gas spring is smooth
- Lighter than Vari (easier to reposition)
- Decent stability for monitors up to 27 inches

**Cons:**
- Noticeable wobble at full height with heavier monitors
- Narrower keyboard tray
- Gas spring can drift slightly over time

### X-shaped vs Z-shaped

FlexiSpot's lineup includes two mechanism styles:
- **X-shaped**: the lifting arms form an X. More compact, slightly less stable at full height.
- **Z-shaped**: arms form a Z. More stable at full extension but takes more desk depth.

For standing desk use, the Z-shaped models (slightly pricier) are worth it if you'll spend significant time standing.

### Limitations

The FlexiSpot's 15-inch range suits users from roughly 5'4" to 6'0". Shorter or taller users may find the top or bottom of the range uncomfortable.

## Premium Pick: Ergotron WorkFit

Ergotron (the monitor arm company) makes a converter focused on long-term durability and ergonomics.

### Build Quality

The WorkFit-TL uses heavier-gauge steel than the competition and a counterbalance mechanism (like their monitor arms) rather than gas springs. This means no drift over time — a common failure point for gas-spring converters.

**Key features:**
- 15-inch height range
- Holds up to 35 lbs
- Counterbalance mechanism (no drift)
- Large keyboard tray (19 x 8 inches)
- Tool-free adjustment
- 5-year warranty

**Price:** $349

### Keyboard Tray Design

The WorkFit's keyboard tray is the largest in this comparison — large enough for a full-size keyboard with numpad plus a mouse. If you've been frustrated by cramped converter trays, this is the fix.

### Long-Term Durability

The counterbalance mechanism and 5-year warranty make the WorkFit the pick for users who plan to use their converter daily for years. Gas springs eventually lose pressure; counterbalance mechanisms don't.

## What to Check Before Buying

### Desk Depth

Converters need desk depth — typically 24-30 inches for the base plus your monitor's depth. Measure your desk before ordering. A converter that overhangs the desk edge is unstable and dangerous.

### Monitor Weight

Add your monitor's weight (check the spec sheet, not the shipping weight) plus any monitor light bar or webcam. Stay under 80% of the converter's rated capacity for stable operation.

### Keyboard Size

If you use a full-size keyboard with numpad, check the tray dimensions carefully. Many converters are designed for tenkeyless keyboards. A numpad that overhangs the tray isn't just annoying — it's unstable.
