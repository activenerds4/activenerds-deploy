## The Ergonomic Stack

### Chair First Philosophy

Your chair is the foundation. If your pelvis and spine aren't supported, no keyboard or mouse will fix the resulting pain. A good chair:
- Supports the natural curve of your spine (lumbar support)
- Allows your feet to rest flat on the floor (seat height adjustment)
- Distributes weight across your thighs (seat pan depth)
- Enables movement (recline, tilt, swivel)

Spend your first dollar here. Everything else builds on top.

### Input Devices Second

Once your posture is supported, address your hands. The keyboard and mouse are where repetitive strain happens. Investing in ergonomic input devices prevents the most common tech injury: RSI.

### Monitor Positioning Third

Your neck follows your eyes. If your monitor is too low, you'll develop forward head posture ("tech neck"). A monitor arm or riser is a cheap fix with high impact.

## Building on a Budget

### Priority Order

If you can't buy everything at once, invest in this order:

1. **Chair** ($300-$500): The foundation — get a Branch or HON Ignition
2. **Monitor arm** ($100-$170): Cheap fix for [neck pain](/articles/desk-stretching-neck-pain) — Ergotron LX
3. **Ergonomic mouse** ($50-$100): Logitech MX Vertical or Microsoft Sculpt mouse
4. **Footrest** ($20-$35): If your feet don't reach the floor
5. **[Ergonomic keyboard](/articles/ergonomic-keyboard-mouse-reviews)** ($80-$400): Microsoft Sculpt or Kinesis Advantage360
6. **Standing desk** ($200-$500): Optional upgrade — see our standing desk guide

### Where to Splurge vs Save

**Splurge:**
- Chair — you spend 8+ hours in it, and back pain is expensive
- Keyboard — if you have wrist pain, a split keyboard is worth the investment

**Save:**
- Monitor arm — a $100 Ergotron is 90% as good as a $400 Humanscale
- Footrest — a $20 IKEA footrest works as well as a $50 premium one
- Mouse — a $50 vertical mouse gives you most of the benefit of a $99 one

### Used Market Tips

Premium chairs hold their value but can be found used:

- **Craigslist/Facebook Marketplace**: Herman Miller and Steelcase chairs from closing offices — often 50-70% off
- **Refurbished**: Herman Miller sells refurbished chairs with full warranty
- **Office liquidators**: Search for "office furniture liquidators" in your area

A used Herman Miller Embody in good condition can be found for $600-$800 — half the new price.
