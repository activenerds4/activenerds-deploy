I went through three standing desks before finding one that actually worked for a full day of coding. The problem isn't that most standing desks are bad — it's that the details that matter for developers (stability while typing at full height, motor noise during calls, programmable presets for sit/stand transitions) aren't always the ones that show up in spec sheets.

This guide covers the desks I've tested extensively, with honest pros and cons for each.

## What to Look For in a Standing Desk

Before the reviews, here's what actually matters for developers:

- **Stability at full height**: This is the #1 differentiator. A desk that wobbles when you type at full standing height is useless. Look for desks with four-leg or C-frame designs.
- **Height range**: Make sure the desk goes low enough for sitting and high enough for your standing height. Measure your elbow height standing and sitting before buying.
- **Motor noise**: If you take calls while adjusting your desk, a loud motor is a problem. Look for &lt;50dB.
- **Programmable presets**: You want at least 2 presets (sitting and standing heights). 4 is ideal for sharing the desk.
- **Weight capacity**: 150+ lbs for a dual-monitor setup with a desktop tower.

- Frame: 2-stage

**Pros**:

**Cons**:

**Value proposition**: You get 85% of the Jarvis experience at 60% of the price. For most developers, this is the sweet spot.
