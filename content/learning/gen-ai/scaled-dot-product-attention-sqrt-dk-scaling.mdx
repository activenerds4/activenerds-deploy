## Question
In the standard scaled dot-product attention formula `Softmax((QK^T) / sqrt(d_k)) * V`, why is the dot product scaled by `sqrt(d_k)`, and what happens to the gradients during backpropagation if this scaling factor is omitted as `d_k` grows large?

## Quick Answer
The `sqrt(d_k)` scaling factor in `Softmax((QK^T) / sqrt(d_k)) * V` keeps the variance of the raw attention scores roughly constant regardless of the key dimension, preventing the softmax from saturating into a near-one-hot distribution.

## Detailed Answer
When query and key vectors have components that are independent random variables with mean 0 and variance 1, the dot product `q · k` sums `d_k` such terms, so its variance grows proportionally to `d_k`. As the head dimension gets larger — which happens naturally as models scale up — the raw dot products get larger in magnitude too, purely as a statistical side effect of summing more terms, not because the attention pattern is actually more confident.

Dividing by `sqrt(d_k)` counteracts exactly this growth: since variance scales with `d_k`, dividing by its square root normalizes the variance back down to roughly 1, keeping the scores in a stable, predictable range no matter how wide the attention heads are.

Without this scaling, large dot products push the **softmax** function into its saturated regions, where it behaves almost like an argmax — assigning nearly all probability mass to a single position and near-zero everywhere else. During backpropagation, the **gradient of softmax** is proportional to `p_i * (1 - p_i)` for each output; when `p_i` is pushed close to 0 or 1, that product collapses toward zero. The result is **vanishing gradients**: the attention weights stop receiving meaningful learning signal, and training becomes unstable or stalls entirely, especially in deeper networks with larger `d_k` per head.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Key Takeaways">
The `sqrt(d_k)` divisor isn't a tuning knob — it's a variance-correction term that keeps softmax gradients alive as attention head dimensions scale up.
</Callout>
