## Question
At a mechanical level, how does an image diffusion model turn random noise into a coherent image, and why is the training objective (predicting noise) different from what happens during inference (iterative denoising)?


## Quick Answer
A diffusion model is trained to solve one narrow task — predict the noise that was added to an image at a given noise level — and inference reuses that same noise-predictor repeatedly, subtracting a small estimated amount of noise at each of many steps until pure random noise gradually resolves into a coherent image.

## Detailed Answer
**Training** works backward from real images. The process, called the **forward diffusion process**, takes a real training image and progressively adds small amounts of Gaussian noise to it over many steps, until after enough steps the image is indistinguishable from pure random noise. Because this noise-adding process is mathematically well-defined, it's possible to compute exactly how much noise was added to produce any intermediate noisy version of the image at any given step. The model is then trained on a simple, well-posed supervised task: given a noisy image and the number of steps of noise that were applied, predict what that added noise actually was. This is a much more tractable training objective than trying to predict an entire clean image directly, since predicting a Gaussian noise pattern is a well-behaved regression problem the network can learn efficiently.

**Inference** runs the same trained noise-predictor in reverse. It starts from pure random noise — no real image involved at all — and repeatedly asks the model "what noise do you think is present in this image at this step," then subtracts a scaled-down estimate of that predicted noise, producing a slightly less noisy image. Repeating this for many steps (the number of steps and the schedule for how much noise to remove each time are configurable, trading generation speed for quality) gradually walks the image from pure static toward something the model judges to be a clean, plausible image consistent with its training distribution — and with a text-conditioning signal like a prompt embedding, toward an image plausible *given that description*, specifically.

The reason the training objective (predicting noise on a known-corrupted real image) differs from what happens at inference (iteratively denoising from pure noise with no real image ever involved) is that diffusion training never has direct access to "generate an image from scratch" as a supervised task — there's no ground-truth target for "what should pure noise become." Instead, training decomposes image generation into thousands of small, well-defined denoising sub-problems that *do* have clear ground truth (the exact noise that was added), and it turns out that chaining together many small, accurate denoising steps at inference time is enough to produce full image generation as an emergent capability, without ever training on that end-to-end task directly.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Key Takeaways">
Diffusion models never learn to generate images directly — they learn to undo one small step of noise at a time, and full image generation emerges from repeating that one narrow skill many times over.
</Callout>
