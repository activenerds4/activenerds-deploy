## Question
How do Temperature, Top-K, and Top-P (Nucleus) sampling mechanically alter the logit probability distribution before token generation, and what combination would you deploy to guarantee deterministic JSON output versus creative text generation?


## Quick Answer
**Temperature** rescales the logits before softmax to flatten or sharpen the probability distribution, **Top-K** restricts sampling to the K highest-probability tokens, and **Top-P (nucleus)** restricts sampling to the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds P — combining them controls how random or how deterministic generation is.

## Detailed Answer
All three mechanisms act on the token probability distribution produced by the final softmax layer, but at different stages.

**Temperature** divides each logit by a value $T$ before softmax is applied: $\text{Softmax}(x_i / T)$. A $T < 1$ sharpens the distribution, making already-likely tokens even more dominant; a $T > 1$ flattens it, giving lower-probability tokens more of a chance. $T \to 0$ pushes the distribution toward a hard argmax; $T = 1$ leaves the original distribution untouched.

**Top-K** operates *after* the probabilities are computed: it zeroes out every token outside the K highest-probability candidates, then renormalizes what remains before sampling. This puts a hard ceiling on how many candidates are ever considered, regardless of how the probability mass happens to be distributed among them.

**Top-P (nucleus sampling)** is adaptive rather than fixed-count: it sorts tokens by probability and keeps adding them to the candidate pool until their cumulative probability crosses threshold $P$, then samples only from that pool. Unlike Top-K, the number of candidates varies turn to turn — a very confident next-token prediction might need only 2 tokens to reach $P = 0.9$, while an uncertain one might need 50.

| Setting                         | Effect                          | Use Case                        |
|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Temperature ≈ 0, Top-K = 1       | Greedy, fully deterministic     | Structured JSON, tool calls, code |
| Temperature ≈ 0.7, Top-P ≈ 0.9   | Moderate variety, mostly coherent | General chat, Q&A                |
| Temperature ≈ 1.0–1.2, Top-P ≈ 0.95 | High variety, more surprising  | Creative writing, brainstorming  |

To guarantee deterministic output for something like strict JSON generation, set temperature near 0 (or use greedy decoding directly) and Top-K to 1 — this collapses the distribution to always pick the single highest-probability token. For creative generation, a moderate-to-high temperature combined with Top-P sampling gives controlled randomness without letting genuinely low-probability, incoherent tokens slip through.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Key Takeaways">
Temperature reshapes the probability curve, while Top-K and Top-P decide how much of that curve is even eligible to be sampled from — deterministic output needs both narrowed to their tightest settings.
</Callout>
