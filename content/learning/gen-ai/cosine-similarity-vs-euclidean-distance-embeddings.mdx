## Question
Mathematically and concept-wise, why is cosine similarity often preferred over Euclidean distance (`L_2`) for high-dimensional text embeddings, and under what normalization condition are their rankings mathematically equivalent?

## Quick Answer
**Cosine similarity** measures the angle between two embedding vectors and ignores their magnitude, which makes it robust to text-length effects that distort raw **Euclidean distance** in high-dimensional space.

## Detailed Answer
An embedding vector's direction tends to encode semantic meaning, while its magnitude often reflects incidental factors like token count or how "intense" the underlying text statistics were during training. Euclidean distance (`L_2`) measures straight-line distance between two points, so it's sensitive to both direction and magnitude — two semantically identical sentences of different length can end up with embeddings of different norms, and Euclidean distance will penalize that difference even though it says nothing about meaning.

Cosine similarity sidesteps this by computing `cos(θ) = (A · B) / (||A|| * ||B||)`, normalizing out the vector lengths and comparing only their orientation. In very high-dimensional spaces (the hundreds or thousands of dimensions typical of text embeddings), this normalization tends to produce more semantically meaningful rankings, because it isolates the "what does this vector point toward" signal from the "how large is this vector" noise.

The two metrics become mathematically equivalent for ranking purposes under one specific condition: **when all vectors are already unit-normalized** (i.e., `||A|| = ||B|| = 1`). In that case, Euclidean distance reduces to `||A - B||^2 = 2 - 2*cos(θ)` — a monotonically decreasing function of cosine similarity, so sorting by smallest Euclidean distance produces the exact same ranking as sorting by highest cosine similarity. This is why many vector databases normalize embeddings at ingestion time and then use the cheaper dot-product or `L_2` index under the hood while effectively delivering cosine-similarity search.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Key Takeaways">
Normalize your embeddings once at write time, and cosine similarity and Euclidean distance become interchangeable — letting you use whichever the vector index implements fastest.
</Callout>
