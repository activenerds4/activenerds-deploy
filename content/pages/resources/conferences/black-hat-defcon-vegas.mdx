# Black Hat USA & DEF CON Guide

**Black Hat and DEF CON run back-to-back in Las Vegas every August — together they're the most important week in security.** Black Hat is the enterprise/vendor conference (briefings, trainings, a real expo); DEF CON is the community/hacker convention (chaotic, cheap, cash-only, and where the real offensive research lands). For DevSecOps and cloud-security engineers, the cloud-native and supply-chain tracks at both are the annual signal.

## 1. Who Should Attend

The two events serve overlapping but distinct audiences.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Best fit: DevSecOps, cloud-security & offensive-security engineers">
If you secure cloud-native workloads, do threat modeling, run red/blue teams, or build supply-chain controls, the cloud-security and DevSecOps tracks at both events are the year's highest-density signal. Black Hat for the enterprise/vendor view; DEF CON for the raw research and community.
</Callout>

- **DevSecOps & cloud-security engineers** — Black Hat's cloud track and DEF CON's cloud villages are the draw.
- **Offensive security / red teamers** — DEF CON's CTFs, villages, and the Black Hat briefings on new attack research.
- **Defensive / blue team & detection engineers** — the detection-engineering and runtime-security content.
- **Supply-chain security folks** — SBOM, signing, and the SLSA content is increasingly prominent at both.

<Callout variant="info" title="Maybe skip if">
You're purely a developer with no security remit — the content assumes security fluency. If you want the applied DevSecOps lens without the offensive depth, fwd:cloudsec (below) is a better, cheaper fit.
</Callout>

## 3. Session Selection Strategy

Both events run parallel tracks; the content is dense and the recordings are uneven.

- **Black Hat:** prioritize the cloud, supply-chain, and DevSecOps briefings; the keynotes are skimmable. Use the expo to meet vendors you're evaluating.
- **DEF CON:** the villages (Cloud, Packet Hacking, etc.) are where the hands-on learning lives — budget time there over the main talks. The main talks are great but recorded for YouTube later.
- **Bring your real architecture.** The cloud villages and Black Hat cloud track speakers will discuss your actual setup if you ask.
- **DEF CON contests (CTF, etc.)** are worth watching even if you don't compete — you'll see real attack patterns.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The cloud villages are the hidden gem">
DEF CON's Cloud Security Village and Black Hat's cloud track are where practitioners share the cloud-native attack and defense research that shapes the year. If you're DevSecOps, this is your highest-ROI time at either event.
</Callout>

## 5. Travel & Accommodation (Las Vegas)

Both events are in Vegas in August — the most physically punishing conference combination on this list.

- **Hotel:** stay at or adjacent to your primary venue (Mandalay Bay for Black Hat, Caesars/LVCC area for DEF CON). The walk between them is long in 40°C+ heat.
- **Flights:** LAS; arrive Friday for Black Hat trainings or Wednesday for DEF CON.
- **Ground transport:** rideshare or monorail; avoid midday outdoor walks in August.
- **Food:** off-Strip is cheaper and better; scout options early.

## 6. Physical Survival & Wellness

Vegas in August plus two back-to-back conferences is a serious wellness and security challenge.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Vegas August + two conferences = a hazard">
Expect 40°C+ outdoors, aggressive AC indoors, and two weeks of dense content if you attend both. Dehydration, sleep debt, and cognitive overload compound. A strict wellness protocol is non-negotiable, not optional.
</Callout>

## 7. Security & Pre-Conference Checklist

Security conferences have unique OPSEC considerations — read this section.

<Callout variant="warning" title="DEF CON OPSEC: assume hostile networks">
On DEF CON networks, assume every packet is monitored. Use a VPN, turn off auto-connect, disable Bluetooth, use a burner phone or faraday bag for sensitive devices, and never use public charging stations (bring your own power bank). This is not paranoia — it's the documented reality of the event.
</Callout>
