# NVIDIA GTC Guide

**GTC is where the hardware that runs your training jobs gets announced.** If you size GPU clusters, optimize inference, or care about the next generation of CUDA and networking, GTC is the source. It's a vendor conference — NVIDIA runs it — but the technical depth in the developer and research tracks is real, and the keynotes set the roadmap every ML infrastructure team will plan against for the next 18 months.

## 1. Who Should Attend

GTC is broad — it spans research, enterprise, robotics, and automotive — but the highest-signal audience is ML infrastructure.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Best fit: ML infrastructure & inference engineers">
If you provision GPUs, tune CUDA kernels, serve LLMs at scale, or plan cluster capacity around the next architecture launch, GTC is your highest-ROI event. The inference-serving and systems tracks are where NVIDIA's own engineers share the optimization details that never make it into docs.
</Callout>

- **ML infrastructure & platform engineers** — GPU scheduling, multi-instance GPU, cluster topology, networking (NVLink, InfiniBand).
- **Inference & serving engineers** — TensorRT, Triton, vLLM, quantization, KV-cache optimization. The serving track is gold.
- **CUDA/kernel developers** — the developer track goes deep on programming models, libraries, and profiling.
- **Research scientists** — the research track and poster sessions cover frontier model and systems work.

<Callout variant="info" title="Maybe skip if">
You're an applied ML practitioner who never touches hardware — GTC is infrastructure-heavy. A domain conference (Databricks, an AI Engineer Summit) will serve you better for applied/pipeline content.
</Callout>

## 3. Session Selection Strategy

GTC runs many parallel tracks. The key is filtering for the systems/infrastructure signal over the marketing.

- **Filter by track, not by title.** The "Systems & Infrastructure" and "Inference & Serving" tracks are where the technical depth lives; the "Enterprise" track is often customer-story marketing.
- **Prioritize NVIDIA-engineer sessions.** Talks by the people who built CUDA, Triton, and the networking stack include details that never reach docs.
- **Watch the keynotes for the roadmap, then skip the rest of the keynote day for labs.** The architecture announcements matter; the partner parade doesn't.
- **The poster session is underrated.** Research posters are where you find the non-marketed systems work — quantization tricks, scheduling heuristics, memory optimizations.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Bring your real problems">
NVIDIA engineers staff the expo and the "meet the expert" desks. Bring a concrete bottleneck — a kernel that won't hit roofline, a serving latency budget you're missing — and you'll often get a direct answer or a contact. This is the highest-leverage 15 minutes of the conference.
</Callout>

## 5. Travel & Accommodation

San Jose in March is mild but the logistics are convention-standard.

- **Hotel:** stay near the San Jose McEnery Convention Center; the walk beats the rideshare queue when thousands exit at once.
- **Flights:** SJC is closest; SFO is a longer transit but often cheaper. Arrive Sunday for Monday keynotes.
- **Ground transport:** Caltrain and VTA light rail connect SJC/SFO to downtown; skip the rental car.
- **Food:** downtown San Jose has good options within walking distance; scout them Sunday.

## 6. Physical Survival & Wellness

GTC is a week of dense, high-cognitive-load content — treat it like a long training run for your brain.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Cognitive overload is the real risk">
GTC sessions are information-dense and the keynotes run long. By Wednesday afternoon, retention collapses if you haven't paced yourself. Schedule breaks the way you'd schedule rest between training epochs.
</Callout>

<ChecklistBox
  title="Conference-week wellness protocol"
  items={[
    "Wear supportive shoes — the San Jose convention center floors are concrete",
    "Alternate sit/stand across sessions; stand at the back for 2 per day",
    "20-20-20 eye reset between sessions — screen-heavy demos strain focus",
    "Hydrate 1:1 with the espresso bar; dry convention air compounds cognitive fatigue",
    "Take a 10-minute outdoor walk at lunch — March in San Jose is pleasant, use it",
    "Cap evening events at 2 nights; the DLI workshops are morning-fresh",
    "Pack a resistance band for thoracic mobility in your room",
    "Treat keynotes like a long training run — hydrate, eat, don't skip sleep the night before",
  ]}
/>
