# DevOpsDays Global Series Guide

**DevOpsDays is the anti-KubeCon: community-run, single-track, cheap, and local.** There's no mega-vendor pavilion, no 12-way parallel schedule, and no $2,000 pass. Instead you get one room, one track of practitioner talks, open-space discussions where the audience sets the agenda, and a crowd that's mostly people doing the actual work in your region. For most practitioners, it's the highest-ROI event of the year.

## 1. Who Should Attend

DevOpsDays is the most accessible technical conference series in the world. The barrier to entry is low and the audience is genuinely mixed.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Best fit: practitioners who want signal, not spectacle">
If you're an SRE, platform engineer, DevOps lead, or security engineer who wants real conversations with peers in your region — not a vendor sales funnel — this is your event. It's also the best first conference for someone who's never attended one.
</Callout>

- **Practitioners at any level** — talks range from beginner to advanced; the single track means you see everything.
- **Local community builders** — if you want to start or grow a local DevOps/K8s meetup, the organizers are here and approachable.
- **First-time conference attendees** — low cost, low pressure, no overwhelming parallel tracks.
- **Speakers cutting their teeth** — DevOpsDays is the friendliest stage to give your first conference talk; the CFP is open and the audience is supportive.

<Callout variant="info" title="Maybe skip if">
You specifically need deep K8s or cloud-native content — DevOpsDays is broader (culture, automation, security, SRE) and shallower on any single topic than KubeCon. Pair it with a domain-specific event.
</Callout>

## 2. Format & What You Get

Each city runs its own event, but the format is consistent worldwide.

- **Single track** — one room, one talk at a time. You see the whole program; no FOMO from parallel sessions.
- **30-minute talks + 5-minute ignite talks** — punchy, practitioner-focused, rarely vendor pitches.
- **Open spaces (unconference)** — the signature format. See below.
- **Meals included** — breakfast and lunch are usually in the ticket price, which is why a $150 pass is a steal.
- **Sponsor booths, not a mega-pavilion** — a handful of local sponsors, low-key, no aggressive swag-hawking.

## 3. Open Spaces: The Real Value

The afternoon open-space sessions are what make DevOpsDays different from every other conference. The audience proposes topics, votes with their feet, and breaks into small discussion groups.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Don't skip the open spaces">
The talks are good, but the open spaces are where you solve actual problems with peers. People bring real incidents, real architecture questions, and real tooling debates. If you sit in the back and scroll, you've wasted the event.
</Callout>

- **Propose a topic.** It doesn't have to be a talk — "how do you handle secrets across 40 clusters?" is a perfect open-space prompt.
- **Vote with your feet.** The rule of open spaces: if a session isn't useful, leave and join another. No offense taken.
- **Take notes and share.** Open-space discussions are rarely recorded; the value is in being there and writing up what you learned for your team.

## 4. Budget & Registration

DevOpsDays is community-priced. The pass is often less than a single night's hotel at KubeCon.

<ChecklistBox
  title="Registration & cost checklist"
  items={[
    "Find your nearest event at devopsdays.org — most cities run once a year",
    "Register early — many regional events sell out (cap is often 200–500 attendees)",
    "Typical pass: $75–$300, usually including breakfast and lunch",
    "Check for a diversity scholarship — most events offer sponsored free passes",
    "If your company sponsors, ask for a comped pass in exchange for a booth shift",
    "Some events offer a virtual/hybrid ticket if you can't travel",
  ]}
/>

<Callout variant="note" title="Submit a talk">
The CFP for each city is open and the bar is friendly. A 30-minute talk on something you actually built — a CI pipeline refactor, an on-call rotation redesign, a security automation win — is exactly what the audience wants. It's the best way to build a speaking resume before aiming at KubeCon.
</Callout>

## 5. Travel & Accommodation

Because events are regional, travel is usually minimal — which is the whole point.

- **Pick the nearest city** — most attendees drive or take a short train. Hotel costs are often the only real expense.
- **Stay near the venue** — regional venues aren't always in walkable districts; check the map before booking.
- **Carpool or coordinate** — local meetup groups often organize shared travel; ask in your region's Slack/Discord.
- **If flying, book early** — regional events sell out hotels faster than they sell out passes.

## 6. Physical Survival & Wellness

DevOpsDays is shorter (usually 1–2 days) and less physically brutal than a mega-conference, but the same rules apply in miniature.

<ChecklistBox
  title="Event-day wellness protocol"
  items={[
    "Wear comfortable shoes — you'll stand a lot during open spaces",
    "Hydrate; the free coffee flows all day, balance it with water",
    "Take the 20-20-20 eye reset between talks",
    "Use the lunch break for a 10-minute outdoor walk — daylight resets focus",
    "Don't hunch over a laptop during open spaces — participate, don't transcribe",
    "If it's a 2-day event, cap the evening social at one night",
  ]}
/>

<Callout variant="tip" title="A great place to test your wellness routine">
Because DevOpsDays is short and local, it's the perfect low-stakes environment to try a conference wellness protocol — the [Desk Stretch Timer](/tools/desk-stretch-timer) or [Focus Session Tracker](/tools/focus-session-tracker) — before deploying it at a week-long event like KubeCon.
</Callout>
