# Standing Desk Transition Protocol Checklist

**Standing all day is just sitting — but for your feet. Transition gradually.**

The most common standing-desk mistake is going from 8 hours of sitting to 8 hours of standing overnight. Your feet, calves, and lower back aren't ready, and you end up sore and back in the chair by Thursday. This checklist paces the transition so you build the capacity to stand without the backlash.

## 1. Week 1–2: Build Tolerance

<ChecklistBox
  title="Week 1–2: Build Tolerance"
  items={[
    "Week 1: stand for 1–2 hours total per day, in short 15–20 minute blocks",
    "Week 2: increase to 2–3 hours per day, still in blocks, never > 30 min at a stretch",
    "Stand only when you feel fresh — don't force standing through fatigue early on",
    "Track standing time (a simple timer or tracker) so you ramp deliberately, not randomly",
    "No standing in the first hour of the day (let your body warm up sitting first)",
    "No standing in the last hour before bed (it can disrupt winding down)",
    "If anything hurts (feet, knees, lower back), reduce standing time and reassess",
  ]}
/>

<Callout variant="warning" title="The transition is the point">
  The goal isn't to stand all day — it's to break up long sitting with standing and movement. People who "graduate" to standing 8 hours often develop new pain (feet, calves, lower back) and quit. The healthy endpoint is a mix: sit, stand, walk, repeat. Don't race to all-standing.
</Callout>

## 2. Sit-Stand Ratio

<ChecklistBox
  title="Sit-Stand Ratio"
  items={[
    "Target ratio once acclimated: roughly 20 minutes standing / 40 minutes sitting per hour",
    "Alternate every 30–60 minutes — set a transition timer (don't rely on remembering)",
    "Stand for active tasks (calls, code review, reading) more than deep-focus writing",
    "Sit for deep-focus, high-precision work where stillness helps concentration",
    "Never stand for > 2 hours straight without a sit or walk break",
    "Total standing per day capped at ~4 hours for most people (not a marathon)",
    "Transition the desk height when you switch — don't stand at sitting height or vice versa",
  ]}
/>

## 3. Anti-Fatigue Mat & Footwear

<ChecklistBox
  title="Anti-Fatigue Mat & Footwear"
  items={[
    "Anti-fatigue mat used whenever standing (cushions feet, encourages micro-movements)",
    "Mat thick enough to compress slightly (3/4 inch typical) but not so soft it's unstable",
    "Supportive footwear — not barefoot or flat slippers on a hard floor",
    "A second pair of shoes kept by the desk if you work barefoot at home",
    "Mat positioned so you stand on it, not beside it (it only works if you're on it)",
    "Consider a balance board or foot roller for occasional active standing (not constant)",
    "Mat cleaned / replaced when it compresses flat and loses cushion",
  ]}
/>

## 4. Weight-Shifting & Movement

<ChecklistBox
  title="Weight-Shifting & Movement"
  items={[
    "Shift weight between feet regularly — don't lock into one stance",
    "Keep a slight bend in the knees — never lock the knees while standing",
    "Use a footrest / stool to prop one foot up alternately (reduces lower-back load)",
    "Calf raises while standing: 10 reps occasionally to keep circulation going",
    "Mini-walks every 20–30 minutes (even to the kitchen and back)",
    "Don't stand perfectly still — micro-movements prevent stiffness and pooling",
    "Stretch calves and hamstrings at the end of standing blocks",
  ]}
/>

<Callout variant="tip" title="One foot up, alternate">
  Propping one foot on a low stool or footrest tilts your pelvis slightly and takes load off the lower back. Alternate feet every few minutes. It's the single biggest comfort upgrade for standing after the mat itself.
</Callout>

## 5. Posture Alignment

<ChecklistBox
  title="Posture Alignment"
  items={[
    "Ears over shoulders over hips over heels — no forward lean into the screen",
    "Desk height set so elbows are ~90 degrees when standing (re-measure — it differs from sitting)",
    "Monitor at arm's length, top bezel at eye level (raise it higher when standing)",
    "Shoulders relaxed and down, not hunched up toward the ears",
    "Core lightly engaged — don't let the belly hang forward or the lower back over-arch",
    "Feet pointed forward, roughly hip-width apart (not turned out or pigeon-toed)",
    "No leaning on the desk with locked elbows — that's just sitting with extra steps",
  ]}
/>

## 6. Listen to the Signals

<ChecklistBox
  title="Listen to the Signals"
  items={[
    "Foot or calf soreness = reduce standing time, check footwear and mat",
    "Lower-back ache = check posture (over-arched?) and add the one-foot-up stance",
    "Knee pain = check for locked knees and reduce standing duration",
    "Neck tension = monitor too low when standing (raise it)",
    "Hip tightness = add calf/hip stretches; you may be over-arching",
    "If pain persists > a few days, back off and reassess setup — don't push through it",
    "Re-evaluate the protocol monthly as your standing tolerance changes",
  ]}
/>
