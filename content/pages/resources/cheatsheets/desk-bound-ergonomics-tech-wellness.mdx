# Desk-Bound Ergonomics & Movement Routines Cheat Sheet

**The body you debug with is the body you ship with.**

## 1. The Desk Setup Math

Get the geometry right once and everything else gets easier. These are the formulas, not the vibes.

### Monitor distance & height

```text
Monitor distance ≈ arm's length (≈ 50–100 cm / 20–40 in)
  → Too close: eye strain, neck forward posture
  → Too far: leaning in, same problem

Monitor top edge ≈ at or slightly below eye level
  → Eyes look slightly downward (15–20° below horizontal)
  → Prevents neck extension (looking up strains neck)

Dual monitors: primary directly in front; secondary at ≤ 35° angle
```

<Callout variant="tip" title="The arm's-length test">
Sit back in your chair and extend your arm — your fingertips should just touch the screen. That's your monitor distance. Closer and you'll crane your neck; farther and you'll lean forward. Set it once; it's worth the 2 minutes.
</Callout>

### Chair & desk height

```text
Desk height ≈ elbow height when seated, forearms parallel to floor
  → Shoulders relaxed, not shrugged
  → Wrists straight (neutral), not bent up or down

Chair seat height: thighs parallel to floor, feet flat
  → Knees at ~90°, hips slightly above knees (opens hip angle)
  → Use a footrest if feet don't reach the floor
```

### Keyboard & mouse placement

```text
Keyboard: centered on your body (home row to sternum)
  → "H" key aligns with your midline
  → Mouse immediately to the side, same plane — no reaching

Elbows ≥ 90°, wrists straight, forearms supported but not compressing the edge
```

## 2. The 20-20-20 Rule & Eye Care

The single most effective eye-strain intervention, and it's free.

```text
Every 20 minutes → look at something 20 feet (6 m) away → for 20 seconds
```

### Why it works

Your eyes' focusing muscles (ciliary muscles) fatigue from sustained near-focus. Looking far relaxes them. Twenty seconds is enough to reset. Set a recurring reminder — this is the highest ROI habit on this sheet.

### Eye-care stack

```text
1. 20-20-20 rule (above) — the foundation
2. Blink deliberately — screen focus cuts blink rate by ~60%
3. Position screen to avoid glare (perpendicular to windows)
4. Bias ambient light brighter than the screen (not the reverse)
5. Consider bias lighting behind the monitor for evening work
```

<Callout variant="warning" title="Blue light isn't the main villain">
Eye strain comes mostly from *reduced blinking* and *sustained near-focus*, not blue light. The 20-20-20 rule and conscious blinking help more than blue-light glasses. Use night-shift modes for sleep, not for eye strain.
</Callout>

## 3. Thoracic Mobility Micro-Resets

The upper back stiffens from hours of forward shoulder posture. These take 60 seconds and undo most of the damage.

### The 60-second thoracic reset

```text
1. Chin tucks (×10): pull head straight back, like making a double chin.
2. Shoulder blade squeezes (×10): pinch shoulder blades together, hold 2s.
3. Seated spinal twist (each side, 20s): twist from the mid-back, not the neck.
4. Cat-cow in the chair (×8): arch and round the spine slowly.
```

### Doorway chest opener

```text
Stand in a doorway, forearms on the frame at 90°, step one foot through.
Lean forward until you feel a stretch across the chest/front shoulders.
Hold 30s each side. Counteracts the rounded-shoulder desk posture.
```

<Callout variant="tip" title="Anchor it to a trigger">
Do the thoracic reset every time you finish a deploy, merge a PR, or end a meeting. Anchoring a 60-second routine to an existing trigger beats relying on willpower. The habit is the whole point.
</Callout>

## 4. Wrist & Forearm Routines

For anyone who types 8+ hours a day, wrist maintenance is preventive maintenance for your career.

### The wrist reset (do at every break)

```text
1. Wrist flexor stretch: arm out, palm up, pull fingers back gently — 20s each.
2. Wrist extensor stretch: arm out, palm down, pull fingers down — 20s each.
3. Wrist circles: 8 each direction, slow and full range.
4. Prayer stretch: palms together, lower slowly below chin — 20s.
5. Fist squeezes (×10): make a fist, open fully, spread fingers wide.
```

### Forearm self-massage

```text
Roll a tennis ball or lacrosse ball along the forearm flexors (palm side)
from wrist to elbow, 30s per arm. Finds and releases tight spots.
```

<Callout variant="warning" title="Numbness is a red flag">
If you get numbness/tingling in your thumb, index, or middle finger — especially at night — that's a sign of carpal tunnel irritation, not just tightness. Fix your setup (wrist neutral, not bent) and see a clinician if it persists. Don't "stretch through" nerve symptoms.
</Callout>

## 5. Hip & Lower-Back Resets

Sitting shortens the hip flexors and flattens the lower-back curve. These reset both.

### Standing hip-flexor stretch

```text
Half-kneel, back knee on a pad. Tuck the pelvis under (squeeze the glute
on the back-leg side), then lean forward slightly. You should feel it in
the front of the hip/thigh of the back leg — NOT the lower back.
Hold 30s each side. Do this 2–3× a day.
```

### Seated figure-4

```text
Sit up straight, cross one ankle over the opposite knee (figure-4).
Keep your back straight and hinge forward from the hips until you feel
a stretch in the glute of the crossed leg. Hold 30s each side.
```

### 90/90 hip reset

```text
Sit on the floor, both knees bent to 90°, one leg in front (shin parallel
to body), one to the side. Lean forward over the front shin, then switch.
Opens both internal and external hip rotation. 30s each side.
```

<Callout variant="tip" title="Hip flexors are the real desk villain">
Hours of sitting keep the psoas and iliacus shortened, which tilts the pelvis and flattens the lower-back curve — the root of most "desk back pain." Stretching the hip flexors (not just the back) fixes the cause, not the symptom.
</Callout>

## 6. The Focus-Block Movement Schedule

Pair movement with how you actually work — deep focus blocks and on-call rotations.

<TabsTrigger value="deepwork">Deep Work (90)</TabsTrigger>
<TabsTrigger value="oncall">On-Call</TabsTrigger>

<TabsContent value="pomodoro">

```text
25 min focus → 5 min break
  Break: stand, 20-20-20 eyes, wrist reset, walk to water
Every 4 cycles → 15 min break
  Long break: thoracic reset + hip-flexor stretch + walk
```

</TabsContent>
<TabsContent value="deepwork">

```text
90 min deep focus → 15–20 min break
  Break: full thoracic reset, hip stretch, hydrate, walk
Cap at 2 deep blocks before a longer movement break
  After 2 blocks: 30 min with a walk + doorway opener
```

</TabsContent>
<TabsContent value="oncall">

```text
During incident: 20-20-20 every 20 min (set hard timer)
Post-incident: full reset — thoracic, wrists, hips, walk 5 min
On-call day: add a 10-min walk every 2 hours even if "too busy"
  → Movement clears stress hormones; you'll resolve faster
```

</TabsContent>
</TabsGroup>

<Callout variant="note" title="Movement improves cognition, not just comfort">
Short walks and mobility breaks increase blood flow and have been shown to improve problem-solving and creative thinking. Movement isn't a break *from* work — it's part of doing good work. Reframe it that way and you'll actually do it.
</Callout>

## 7. Daily Minimum Checklist

If you do nothing else, do this. It's the 80/20 of desk wellness.

<ChecklistBox title="The daily minimum" items={[
  "✅ **20-20-20 rule** — every 20 min, 20s, 20 feet. Set the timer.",
  "✅ **Stand every 30 min** — even 30 seconds resets circulation.",
  "✅ **One thoracic reset** — chin tucks + blade squeezes + twist (60s).",
  "✅ **One hip-flexor stretch** — 30s each side, ideally standing.",
  "✅ **Wrist reset at every break** — stretches + circles (60s).",
  "✅ **Hydrate** — water at the desk; getting up to refill is movement.",
  "✅ **One real walk** — 10+ min outside, ideally after lunch.",
  "✅ **Setup check** — monitor at eye level, wrists neutral, feet flat."
]} />

<Callout variant="tip" title="Start with one, not eight">
Don't try to adopt all eight at once. Pick the 20-20-20 rule this week. Add the thoracic reset next week. Habits compound; overwhelm doesn't. The goal is a body that's still comfortable at 60, not a perfect Tuesday.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="warning" title="Pain that persists > 2 weeks">
This sheet prevents and manages common desk stiffness — it's not medical advice. Sharp pain, numbness, or symptoms lasting more than two weeks need a clinician, not a stretch. Catching repetitive-strain issues early is dramatically cheaper than fixing them late.
</Callout>
