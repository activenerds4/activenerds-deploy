**Design for Recoverability**

→ Verify product names, packaging and supported patterns against the current VCF 9.0 documentation and exam guide. DR tooling has been repackaged over time: Site Recovery Manager (SRM) and vSphere Replication are now commonly delivered under the **VMware Live Recovery** family (e.g., Live Site Recovery, Live Cyber Recovery). Confirm current names, which components are included in your VCF entitlement, and the supported way to protect each VCF management component.

---

## 0. Recoverability Fundamentals

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Recoverability (R in AMPRS)** | Ability to **restore service and data** after a failure, disaster, corruption or attack |
| **Business Continuity (BC)** | Keeping critical business functions running through disruption (broader than IT: people, process, facilities, IT) |
| **Disaster Recovery (DR)** | IT-specific recovery of systems/data at an alternate site or from copies after a major outage |
| **Availability vs Recoverability** | Availability **prevents or limits** outage (HA, stretched cluster); recoverability **restores** after outage (backup, replication, DR) |
| **RPO** | Recovery Point Objective: **maximum tolerable data loss** (time) |
| **RTO** | Recovery Time Objective: **maximum tolerable downtime** |
| **MTD / MAO** | Maximum tolerable downtime; RTO must be less than MTD |
| **WRT** | Work Recovery Time: time to validate/resume business after systems return |
| **BIA** | Business Impact Analysis: identifies critical processes, impact, RPO/RTO per service |
| **Tiering** | Group workloads by criticality (e.g., Tier 0 to Tier 3) with different RPO/RTO and cost |

**Rules of thumb**
- **Lower RPO/RTO = higher cost and complexity.** Match protection level to business value (BIA).
- RPO drives **replication/backup frequency and technology**; RTO drives **recovery method, automation, capacity at recovery site**.
- Replication is **not** backup: it copies corruption and deletions too. Keep **backups (ideally immutable)**.

## 1. Recovery Technology Options

| Technology | RPO | RTO | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vSAN stretched cluster** (availability) | ≈0 | Minutes (HA restart) | Site failure tolerance; **not** backup or protection against corruption |
| **Array-based replication (sync)** | ≈0 | Minutes to hours (with orchestration) | Requires supported arrays and SRA with SRM |
| **Array-based replication (async)** | Minutes to hours | Minutes to hours | Array-managed; storage vendor dependent |
| **vSphere Replication (host-based, async)** | Minutes (commonly ≥5 min, verify) | Minutes to hours | Storage-agnostic, per-VM; works with vSAN and others |
| **Site Recovery Manager / Live Site Recovery** | Depends on replication | **Orchestrated, automated, testable** recovery plans | Runbook automation, non-disruptive testing, re-IP, failback, reprotect |
| **Backup (image-level)** | Hours to a day (backup interval) | Hours to days | Protects against corruption/deletion; slower recovery |
| **Immutable/air-gapped backup or cyber recovery vault** | Backup interval | Hours to days | Ransomware resilience; clean recovery copy |
| **Application-level replication** (DB log shipping, Always On, clustering) | Seconds to minutes | Minutes | App-aware, often complements infrastructure DR |

**Key takeaway:** a complete design usually layers **HA** (local), **replication/DR** (site loss), and **backup/immutable copies** (corruption, ransomware).

## 2. BCDR: Management Components vs Workload Domains

### 2.1 Comparison

| Aspect | **VCF Management Components** | **Workload Domains (applications/VMs)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What is protected** | SDDC Manager, vCenter (management and workload), NSX Managers, VCF Operations (+ fleet mgmt), VCF Automation, logs/networks, identity broker, supporting appliances | Tenant/application VMs, Kubernetes workloads, data |
| **Who defines RPO/RTO** | Platform/infrastructure owner (derived from the **platform's** role: needed to operate, recover and manage workloads) | **Business/application owners** per BIA (tiered) |
| **Primary strategy** | **Configuration/file-based backups** of each component (SDDC Manager, vCenter, NSX) to an external backup target, plus **VM-level backup** where supported; **restore-to-rebuild** procedures; selective HA/stretch for management domain | **Replication + orchestrated DR** (SRM/Live Site Recovery, vSphere Replication, array replication), backups, application-level DR |
| **DR to a second site** | Typically **not** replicated like workloads: management plane at DR site is usually a **separate VCF instance/management domain** with its own management components, rather than failing over the production management VMs (verify supported patterns) | Replicated per-VM/protection group to the recovery site; recovered into the DR instance's workload domain |
| **Ordering/dependency** | Strict **recovery sequence**: core services (DNS, NTP, identity) → management domain hosts/vSAN → vCenter → NSX Managers → SDDC Manager → VCF Operations/Automation, etc. (follow documented order) | Dependency-based recovery plans: infrastructure services → databases → app tiers → web tiers |
| **Test approach** | Periodic **restore tests** (e.g., restore into isolated environment), validate backup integrity and runbooks | **Non-disruptive DR tests** (SRM test recovery in isolated network), scheduled failover tests |
| **Backup consistency** | Per-product **supported backup method** (not arbitrary VM snapshots for NSX/SDDC Manager) | Application-consistent backup/replication (quiescing, VSS, app-aware) |
| **Typical RPO/RTO** | Backup-interval RPO (e.g., hours); RTO hours (rebuild/restore) | Tiered: Tier 0 RPO≈0/RTO minutes; Tier 3 RPO 24 h/RTO days |
| **Failure impact** | Loss of management plane does **not** immediately stop running VMs, but blocks **operations, automation, lifecycle, new deployments, and (for NSX) can affect network changes** | Loss directly impacts **business services** |
| **Availability layer** | Management domain HA (N+1), NSX Manager 3-node cluster, anti-affinity, optional stretched management domain | HA, FT, stretched cluster per tier |

### 2.2 Management-Component Recoverability Decisions

| Decision | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Backup target** | External, **separate failure domain** (not on the same vSAN datastore), SFTP/NFS/object as supported; replicate offsite |
| **Backup scope** | SDDC Manager, vCenter(s), NSX Manager(s), VCF Operations/Automation and fleet components, identity broker, configuration/certificates, inventory/credentials documentation |
| **Backup frequency** | Aligned to change rate and acceptable config loss (e.g., daily, plus before any change/upgrade) |
| **Retention** | Multiple restore points; compliance retention |
| **Encryption & access** | Encrypted backups, restricted access, immutable/offsite copy for ransomware |
| **Restore runbook** | Documented, tested order of recovery with dependencies and credentials escrow |
| **Management domain resilience** | Host failures: HA + vSAN FTT; site failure: stretched management domain (if supported/required) **or** DR instance with rebuilt/restored management components |
| **Fleet considerations** | Per-instance management stack: each instance needs its own backup/recovery plan; fleet management and operations components also need protection |
| **Core services** | Redundant DNS/NTP/AD/CA at DR site, required **before** management recovery |
| **Pre-change backups** | Always back up before upgrades/lifecycle operations (ties to Objective 3.6) |
| **Testing** | Test restoring each component periodically and verify version/BOM compatibility (restores must match supported builds) |

### 2.3 Workload-Domain Recoverability Decisions

| Decision | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Classification** | BIA-driven tiering with RPO/RTO per tier; document in design decisions |
| **Replication technology** | By RPO: sync/stretched (≈0), array async or vSphere Replication (minutes), backup (hours+) |
| **Orchestration** | **SRM / Live Site Recovery** recovery plans: ordering, priority, scripts, re-IP, test/cleanup, reprotect/failback |
| **Network at DR site** | Pre-built NSX segments/T0/T1 and security policy at DR (via NSX Federation or automation); IP strategy: stretched networks vs re-IP mapping; DNS updates; external connectivity (BGP/WAN) |
| **Recovery capacity** | DR site hosts/storage sized for **protected workloads only** (N:M ratio); consider active/active vs active/passive vs pilot-light trade-off |
| **Recovery site topology** | Separate VCF instance with workload domain(s) mirrored in design (versions/BOM aligned, similar networking/storage features) |
| **Consistency groups / protection groups** | Group dependent VMs for consistent recovery |
| **Backup** | Application-consistent, immutable copy for ransomware; separate from replication |
| **Cyber recovery** | Isolated recovery environment/clean-room validation before reconnecting recovered VMs |
| **Testing cadence** | Scheduled non-disruptive tests (e.g., quarterly); full failover exercise per policy |
| **Failback** | Reprotect and planned failback plan; bandwidth/time for reverse replication |
| **Kubernetes/modern apps** | Protect via application-aware backup/GitOps/cluster redeploy; PV data protection |

## 3. Business Continuity (RPO/RTO) Design Decisions

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **BIA**: identify critical business services, dependencies, impact of downtime/data loss |
| 2 | Set **RPO/RTO per tier** (and MTD); get stakeholder sign-off |
| 3 | Map tiers to **technologies** (stretched, SRM + replication, backup) |
| 4 | Size **replication bandwidth** (change rate × RPO), recovery capacity, DR network |
| 5 | Define **dependencies and recovery order** (core services first) |
| 6 | Document **runbooks**, roles, communication/escalation, decision authority to declare disaster |
| 7 | **Test** regularly; measure achieved RTO/RPO; remediate gaps |
| 8 | Review after changes (new workloads, version updates, topology change) |

**Example tiering**

| Tier | Example workloads | RPO | RTO | Typical design |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 / Mission-critical** | Payments, core DB | ≈0 | Minutes | Stretched cluster (metro) or sync replication + automated recovery, plus backups |
| **1 / Business-critical** | ERP, CRM | ≤15 min | ≤1-4 h | vSphere Replication/array async + SRM plans |
| **2 / Important** | Internal apps | ~1-4 h | ~8-24 h | Async replication or frequent backup + restore |
| **3 / Non-critical** | Dev/test | 24 h | Days | Backup only (or none) |

**Bandwidth rule of thumb:** replication link must carry the **change rate** (peak) within the RPO window; undersized links = RPO breaches.

## 4. DR Strategy Patterns

| Pattern | Description | RTO | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Active/Active (stretched or app-level)** | Both sites run production | Lowest | Highest | Needs low latency (stretched) or app architecture |
| **Active/Passive (warm standby)** | DR site idle with capacity; replication running; SRM-orchestrated failover | Minutes to hours | High | Common SRM pattern |
| **Pilot light** | Minimal core services running; scale up at disaster | Hours | Medium | Capacity must be obtainable at DR |
| **Cold standby / rebuild from backup** | Hardware or cloud provisioned after disaster; restore from backup | Days | Lowest | Longest RTO |
| **Reciprocal (active/active DR)** | Each site is DR for the other with spare capacity | Low | Medium-high | Capacity at each site for both |
| **Cloud-based DR** | Recover into a cloud VCF/VMware-based target (as offered) | Hours | Variable | Evaluate network, licensing, data sovereignty |

## 5. Scenario Cheat Sheet

| Scenario | Design decision |
|---|---|
| Two metro sites, Tier 0 app needs RPO≈0, auto restart | **vSAN stretched cluster** (+ backups for corruption) |
| Distant DR site, RPO 15 min, orchestrated tested failover | **vSphere Replication + SRM/Live Site Recovery**, recovery plans, DR instance |
| Enterprise array already replicates synchronously | **Array replication + SRA + SRM**; verify supported arrays |
| Ransomware is the primary concern | **Immutable/air-gapped backups**, isolated recovery environment, cyber recovery workflow; replication alone is insufficient |
| Management domain lost, workloads still running | Restore management components from **external backups** in documented order; core services first |
| Need DR for management plane at second site | Separate **DR VCF instance management stack**; backup/restore or rebuild pattern (verify supported alternatives) |
| Backups stored on same vSAN datastore as the VMs | **Design flaw**: backup target must be in a **separate failure domain** |
| DR site hardware smaller than production | Protect **tiered subset**; define which tiers are recovered; document accepted risk |
| Recovery plan never tested | Risk: unvalidated RTO; implement **scheduled non-disruptive tests** |
| Recovered VMs need same IPs at DR | Stretched networks, or SRM **re-IP mapping**, DNS update, pre-built NSX segments |
| Version mismatch between sites | Align BOM/versions; mismatched versions risk failed recoveries |
| Compliance requires data kept in-country | DR site/instance in same jurisdiction; check cloud DR residency |
| RPO 5 min requirement but WAN link is saturated | Increase bandwidth, reduce change rate, or revise RPO; document trade-off |

## 6. Recoverability Trade-offs (AMPRS Lens)

| Choice | Improves | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Lower RPO (sync/stretched) | R, A | Cost, latency/bandwidth dependency, complexity (M), write latency (P) |
| Dedicated DR site/instance | R | Capital and operational cost, lifecycle overhead (M) |
| Orchestrated DR (SRM) | R (predictable RTO), M (testing) | Licensing, runbook upkeep |
| Immutable backups/vault | R, S | Storage cost, slower recovery |
| Frequent backups | R (lower RPO) | Storage/network/performance impact |
| Tiered protection | Cost efficiency | Needs BIA discipline and governance |

---

## 7. Exam Traps

- **Replication ≠ backup**; stretched cluster ≠ DR for corruption/ransomware.
- **Management components and workloads have different strategies**: management = **supported backups + restore/rebuild** (and separate DR-site management stack); workloads = **replication + SRM orchestration**.
- **Backup target in the same failure domain** as the source is invalid.
- **RTO includes more than failover**: detection, decision, recovery, validation (WRT).
- **RPO is limited by replication interval and bandwidth**, not just technology.
- **Untested DR = unproven DR**; include test plans and cadence.
- **Core services (DNS, NTP, identity) must be recovered first**.
- **Recovered environment must match supported versions**: restores must align with the VCF BOM.
- RPO/RTO come from the **business (BIA)**, not from what is technically convenient.

---

## 8. Quick Recall

- **RPO = data loss tolerance; RTO = downtime tolerance**; set per tier from BIA; lower values cost more.
- **Layered protection**: HA (host) → stretched cluster (site, RPO≈0) → replication + SRM (DR, orchestrated) → backups/immutable copies (corruption, ransomware).
- **Management components**: external **supported backups**, strict recovery order, restore testing, separate management stack at DR site (verify patterns).
- **Workload domains**: tiered replication + **SRM/Live Site Recovery** plans, DR network pre-built, capacity sized for protected tiers, non-disruptive tests.
- **Always**: separate failure domain for backups, test regularly, size bandwidth to change rate, align versions, document runbooks.
