## Objective 3.1: Gathering and Analyzing Business Objectives and Requirements

### 3.1.1 The Flow

```
Business drivers → Business objectives → Business requirements
   → (analysis) → Technical requirements, Constraints, Assumptions, Risks
   → VCF 9.0 design goals (AMPRS-aligned)
```

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Business driver** | External/internal force motivating change | Cost pressure, regulation, cloud repatriation, M&A, faster time-to-market, vendor/licence changes |
| **Business objective** | Strategic, measurable goal the business wants | "Cut infrastructure cost 20% in 18 months" |
| **Business requirement** | What the solution must deliver to meet the objective (solution-agnostic) | "Developers get environments within hours, not weeks" |
| **Technical requirement** | Testable capability derived from a business requirement | "Self-service provisioning with approval workflow; &lt;1 hour deploy time" |

### 3.1.2 Gathering Techniques

| Technique | Use for |
|---|---|
| **Stakeholder interviews** | Strategy, priorities, pain points (executives, app owners, security, operations, finance) |
| **Workshops** | Resolving conflicts, building consensus, prioritising |
| **Questionnaires/surveys** | Broad, consistent input from many stakeholders |
| **Document review** | Existing policies, SLAs, architecture docs, audit findings, compliance standards |
| **Current-state assessment / discovery** | Inventory, utilisation, dependencies, capacity (e.g., via VCF Operations data or assessment tools) |
| **Observation / process walkthrough** | Real operational practices vs documented ones |

### 3.1.3 Who to Engage

- **Executive sponsor**: objectives, budget, priorities
- **Business/application owners**: workload needs, SLAs
- **Infrastructure/operations**: operability, skills, tooling
- **Security/compliance/audit**: regulatory and policy requirements
- **Network, storage, DR, finance teams**: dependencies, cost models

### 3.1.4 Analysing Requirements

1. **Classify**: business vs technical; functional vs non-functional (AMPRS qualities).
2. **Clarify** ambiguity and make requirements **specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, testable**.
3. **Prioritise** (must / should / could); resolve conflicts with stakeholders.
4. **Identify** constraints, assumptions, risks; validate assumptions.
5. **Trace**: give each requirement an ID so design decisions can map to it later.
6. **Get sign-off** on the requirements baseline.

### 3.1.5 Translating Business Drivers into VCF 9.0 Design Goals

| Business driver / objective | Design goal | VCF 9.0 architectural implications |
|---|---|---|
| **Reduce cost / TCO**, consolidate sprawl | Standardise and consolidate on one private cloud platform; raise utilisation | Standard VCF domains, HCI (vSAN), right-sized clusters, VCF Operations for capacity/cost optimisation, consolidated architecture for small sites |
| **Faster delivery / agility** | Self-service, automation | **VCF Automation**, templates/blueprints, API/IaC, workload domains per consumer group, Kubernetes via VKS |
| **Regulatory compliance / data sovereignty** | Isolation, auditability, location control | Separate workload domains or instances per region, NSX micro-segmentation, encryption, RBAC, **VCF Identity Broker**/SSO, logging (VCF Operations for logs), compliance monitoring |
| **Business continuity / high uptime** | High availability, defined RTO/RPO | Clustered management domain, vSphere HA, vSAN failure tolerance, stretched cluster or multi-instance with DR, redundant NSX Edge |
| **Growth / scalability** | Predictable scale-out | Scalable domains/clusters, modular workload domains, capacity planning, fleet model for multiple sites |
| **Security / zero-trust posture** | Defence in depth | NSX distributed firewall, segmentation, hardening, certificate and identity management |
| **Operational efficiency, skills gap** | Simplify operations | Centralised lifecycle (SDDC Manager, fleet management in VCF Operations), standardised architecture, automation, validated patterns |
| **Modern applications / AI** | Platform for containers and new workloads | VKS, GPU-capable hosts, private AI services (where in scope) |
| **Cloud repatriation / hybrid strategy** | Consistent operating model across sites/clouds | VCF as common stack, HCX or replication-based migration |
| **M&A / site consolidation** | Integrate quickly, migrate workloads | Brownfield convert/import, migration tooling, multi-instance fleet |

### 3.1.6 Key Takeaways (3.1)

- Business requirements are about **outcomes**, not technology; do not let a product choice appear in a business requirement.
- **Every technical requirement and later design decision must trace to a business requirement.**
- A requirement is **testable** only if it is measurable (numbers for RTO/RPO, uptime %, deploy time, capacity growth).
- **Conflicting requirements** (e.g., high availability vs low cost) are resolved by stakeholder prioritisation, and the **trade-off is documented**.
- **Constraints** come from outside (budget, existing hardware, policy, skills, timeline, legal); the architect designs *within* them.
- **Unvalidated assumptions** must be confirmed or logged as risks.
- Gathering is **iterative**: incomplete or unclear requirements mean going back to stakeholders, not guessing.

## Objective 3.2: Creating a Conceptual Model from Business Objectives

### 3.2.1 What a Conceptual Model Is

- **Highest-level** view of the solution: *what* it provides and *why*; **technology- and vendor-neutral** (or only broad-category).
- Audience: **business stakeholders and executives**, then used as the foundation for the logical design.
- Shows **capabilities, major components/services, actors, relationships and boundaries**, not sizing, IPs, versions or product configuration.

### 3.2.2 Typical Contents

| Element | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope and boundaries** | What is in and out | "Private cloud for production and dev workloads at two data centres; public cloud and end-user computing out of scope" |
| **Actors / consumers** | Who uses the platform | Developers, application owners, operations, security, auditors |
| **Capabilities / services** | What the platform provides | Compute, storage, networking and security services; self-service provisioning; monitoring; backup/DR; identity and access |
| **Major building blocks** | Abstract components | Management layer, workload layer, automation layer, operations layer, security layer |
| **Relationships / interactions** | How blocks and actors connect | Consumers request via self-service → automation provisions on infrastructure → operations monitors |
| **Principles / guiding design goals** | Direction from objectives | "Standardise", "automate by default", "secure by design", "site-resilient" |
| **Constraints, assumptions, risks** | High-level, carried from analysis | "Budget capped", "existing network ready", "skills gap" |

### 3.2.3 Mapping Business Objectives → Conceptual Model → VCF 9.0 (Next Steps)

| Business objective | Conceptual element | Becomes (logical/physical, later) |
|---|---|---|
| Faster provisioning | Self-service consumption layer | VCF Automation, templates, workload domains |
| Regulatory isolation | Security and isolation zones | Separate domains/instances, NSX segmentation, identity broker |
| High availability / DR | Resilience capability across sites | Clusters with HA, stretched cluster or multi-instance plus DR |
| Cost efficiency | Standardised, shared infrastructure pool | HCI with vSAN, consolidated or standard architecture, capacity management |
| Operational simplicity | Centralised management and monitoring | SDDC Manager, VCF Operations (fleet management), VCF Operations for logs/networks |

### 3.2.4 Process to Build It

1. Start from **validated business objectives and requirements** (3.1 output).
2. Define **scope and boundaries**.
3. Identify **actors and capabilities** needed.
4. Group capabilities into **abstract building blocks/layers**.
5. Draw **relationships and flows** between blocks and actors.
6. Add **guiding principles, constraints, assumptions, risks**.
7. **Review with business stakeholders** and obtain agreement before moving to logical design.

### 3.2.5 Key Takeaways (3.2)

- The conceptual model is **derived from business objectives**, not from hardware or product choice.
- **No product versions, IP addresses, VLANs, host counts or sizing**; those belong in the **physical design**. Components and their relationships (without vendor detail) belong in the **logical design**.
- It must be **understandable to non-technical stakeholders** and **traceable** to objectives.
- It is the **bridge**: Business requirements → **Conceptual** → Logical → Physical.
- Stakeholder agreement on the conceptual model **reduces rework** later.
- Mentioning broad VCF 9.0 capability categories (private cloud platform with compute, storage, network, automation, operations) is acceptable; naming specific configuration is not.

## Practical Exam Scenarios

| # | Scenario | Best answer / reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Executive says "We must cut datacentre cost by 20% and speed up provisioning." Which is the business requirement? | The cost and provisioning outcomes (not "deploy vSAN" or "use VCF Automation"), because requirements are outcomes; technologies come later |
| 2 | A statement reads "Use NSX distributed firewall for micro-segmentation." Classify it. | **Technical requirement** (or design decision); it names a technology |
| 3 | "The project must finish before the lease on the old DC ends in 9 months." | **Constraint** (timeline) |
| 4 | "We assume the network team will provide 25 GbE links." | **Assumption**; validate or record as a risk |
| 5 | Two stakeholders request conflicting needs (maximum availability vs minimal cost). | Facilitate a **prioritisation workshop**, document the **trade-off** and decision with sponsor approval |
| 6 | Requirements are vague ("fast and secure"). | **Clarify** with stakeholders into measurable, testable requirements before designing |
| 7 | Which artefact would you show executives to confirm you understand the goals? | **Conceptual model** |
| 8 | A diagram shows host counts, VLAN IDs, and ESXi build numbers. Which level is it? | **Physical design**, not conceptual |
| 9 | Data must remain in-country across two regions. | Compliance requirement drives **separate instances/domains per region**, controlled access and logging; document as a design goal in the conceptual model (isolation by region) |
| 10 | Customer wants "private cloud with self-service and strong governance." | Conceptual layers: consumption/automation, governance/security, infrastructure, operations → later mapped to VCF Automation, NSX/identity, workload domains, VCF Operations |
| 11 | The requirements baseline changes mid-project. | Follow **change control**, update traceability, reassess impact on risks and design |
| 12 | No existing documentation about the current environment. | Perform **current-state discovery/assessment** to gather utilisation and dependency data |

## Quick Recall Sheet

- **Driver → Objective → Business requirement → Technical requirement → Design goal.**
- Gather via **interviews, workshops, surveys, document review, discovery**; engage **all stakeholder groups**.
- Requirements must be **measurable and testable**; assumptions **validated**; conflicts **prioritised and documented**.
- **Conceptual model** = what and why, vendor-neutral, for business audience; **no sizing, IPs, versions**.
- Flow: **Business → Conceptual → Logical → Physical**; keep **traceability** throughout.
- Typical VCF 9.0 translations: cost → standardisation and HCI; agility → VCF Automation; compliance → domain/instance isolation, NSX, identity; availability → HA/stretched/DR; operations → fleet and lifecycle management.
